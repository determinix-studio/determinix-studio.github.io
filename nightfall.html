A box he was told never to open

Milo never met his grandfather — only a name, a few photographs, and a sealed box handed down with one rule: nobody opens it. Eleven and unwatched, he opened it anyway. Under the ordinary keepsakes was an object that shouldn't be warm. He didn't decide to keep it; it just ended up an inch from his pillow.

That night, he didn't dream. He fell past dreaming altogether — down into the Undermind, the shared floor beneath every sleeping mind, where something has been running all along.

A machine no one can explain

It draws a slow current up out of the dark and works it, tirelessly, into the shapes it sends against him. Old, patient, entirely unbothered — where it came from, what it wants, how long it has been down there, no one knows. It has never once had to answer to anyone else being here. It hasn't noticed Milo yet.

He wasn't the first one down

A path worn into flawless machinery says someone stood here before him — and somehow he knows it was the grandfather he never met. In the waking world he finds the proof: a hidden workbench, an old radio still tuned to something, a logbook decades deep. The man who “did some work” in his sleep had spent a lifetime learning the one thing the Machine never could.

Getting stronger arms them, too

Break one of the Machine's shapes and it loses nothing — what falls settles into a raw residue it draws back up and works into the next one, sturdier, unless Milo catches that residue first and refines it into power of his own. Every step he climbs, the Machine climbs too. The only question is who keeps the current — and that choice is the whole game.

“I'm not sorry I found it. I'm only sorry I never made it safe enough to hand to anyone I loved.”
— the last page of his grandfather's logbook

You take his place. Every night, you go under.
